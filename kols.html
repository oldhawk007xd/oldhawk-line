KOL Watch

Who is calling, and their record.

Before you buy what an account is shilling, look at what happened the last times. Every score comes from documented on-chain investigations, court filings and the account's own posts, all linked.

Loading the record

The risk scale.

From 1, a record of holding what they call, to 10, a record of selling into the people who bought. Accounts without a record stay at 5 until their calls are measured.

The accounts.

Riskiest first. Each card shows the record behind its score, with the source of every line.

Every documented incident.

One dot per incident, sized by the amount its source gives. Red: sold into their own promotion. Amber: insiders held or sold the supply. Violet: paid without saying so. Grey: collapsed after the promotion. Green rings: held what they called.

Latest calls, measured.

Every coin a tracked account names on X, by contract address or by the ticker of a runner, from the market cap when we first saw the post, followed for 31 days.

How the score works.

One method for every account, published in full, so anyone can check a score against its sources.

A score describes the risk to buyers when an account promotes a coin, from what is documented. It is not a legal finding: allegations are marked as allegations and answers are quoted. Corrections with a source are welcome on X.
